About

In my research, I want to understand how AI works, and what it might mean for our future. AI models are one instance of a larger challenge: many complex systems, natural and human-made, still resist understanding, and AI agents will create many more. I want to find the mathematical theories, and the new tools, that can explain them. I am also thinking about open-ended learning: how a learner, human or machine, decides what is worth pursuing, as mathematicians do when they build theories. My background is in theoretical computer science and learning theory. I joined Microsoft Research in 2009. Before that I was a postdoc at Georgia Tech and at McGill University. I received my PhD from Rutgers University, advised by Michael Saks. I write about some of my work on the Research page; there is more about me in my CV.
